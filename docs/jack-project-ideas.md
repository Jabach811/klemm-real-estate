# Project Ideas for Jack — Showcase Pile

Purpose: show Jack the range. Not just websites — apps, data toys, tools, print.
Meant as springboards. He sees what's possible, his own ideas start firing.

Written 2026-08-31.

---

## Round One

### Things that look like magic but are actually simple

**The Altamont Map.** Animated map showing where his buyers come from. Dots leave the
Bay Area and land in his cities over a 12-month scrub bar. He already believes this
story — seeing it move makes it a sales tool.

**Commute Cost Calculator.** Buyer types their Bay Area work address. Shows the drive,
the monthly gas + tolls + hours-of-life cost, and what that same money buys in Manteca
vs. staying put.

**"What Can You Actually Afford Out Here" slider.** One slider — their Bay Area rent.
Fills in with real homes in his cities at that monthly payment. No forms, no signup.

**Sold Price Time Machine.** Pick a street, drag a year slider, watch prices bloom on
the map. Uses his own past sales. 25 years of data nobody's ever looked at visually.

### Actual apps (the "not just websites" proof)

**Open House Sign-In on an iPad.** Visitors tap name and phone, lands in a list on his
phone before they leave the driveway. Works with no internet.

**Listing Presentation on his phone.** Swipeable kitchen-table deck — comps, the
Altamont slide, testimonials, live "here's what your home is worth" moment.

**Voice Note to Property Description.** He talks for 45 seconds walking a house. Out
comes a clean listing description and three social captions.

**Flyer Machine.** Paste a listing, pick a template, get a print-ready flyer and a
matching Instagram square in ten seconds.

### Pure showoff pieces

**A neighborhood you can walk through.** Tiny 3D block of Del Webb or River Islands you
can drag around in a browser, click a house, see it sold. Zero practical purpose.
Enormous "oh" factor.

**The Squiggle Dashboard, live.** His analytics but designed — big honest numbers, plain
English sentences instead of charts. Reporting as a product.

**Newsletter Archive as an experience.** His 2009-onward shoebox as a scrolling timeline
with the market chart running behind it.

**A game — "Guess the Sold Price."** Photo of a real past sale, guess, see how close.
Post it weekly. Toy, marketing, and proof you build interactive things for fun.

### The unfair-advantage ones

**Relocation Trigger Watch.** Notices when a big Bay Area employer announces layoffs or
a return-to-office change, pings Jack. The squiggle, automated.

**Neighborhood Fact Sheets, auto-built.** Feed it a community name, it assembles
schools, HOA, commute, price bands, recent sales into a clean one-pager.

**"Should I Sell Now" quiz.** Six questions, honest answer at the end — sometimes
"probably not yet." The honesty is the hook.

## Round Two

### Making the move feel less scary

**The Real Cost of Living Out Here.** Side by side: groceries, gas, utilities, property
tax, water, insurance, garbage. Bay Area column vs. Tracy column.

**A Day In The Life, hour by hour.** Slider from 5am to 9pm comparing a Tracy commuter's
day to a Bay Area renter's day. Same job, same salary, two different Tuesdays.

**"Where do I buy X?" map.** The petty stuff that panics people moving out — nearest
Trader Joe's, urgent care, In-N-Out, dog park, decent sushi, Costco.

**School Compare.** Two schools side by side in plain English, not a rating number.

**The First 90 Days checklist.** DMV, utilities, garbage day, where the lines are
shortest. Makes Jack the guy who helped, not the guy who sold.

### Seller-side (where his money is)

**"What's my home worth" that isn't a lead trap.** Real answer up front, then offers
Jack. Honesty as the differentiator against Zillow.

**Net Sheet Calculator.** Sale price in, actual take-home out — commission, fees,
payoff, closing costs. Asked at every kitchen table.

**Before/After slider on his past listings.** Drag across the photo, staging appears.
Costs nothing, sells his prep work instantly.

**"Should I fix it or sell as-is?"** Few questions about the house, honest read on
whether that $12k kitchen pays back.

**Days-on-Market Reality Check.** His listings vs. market average over years. If his
numbers are good, most convincing thing on the site.

### Trust and personality

**The 25 Years timeline.** Scroll through his career — first sale, the crash, the boom,
the pandemic — with the market chart underneath.

**Testimonial wall that doesn't look fake.** Real handwriting, real photos, sorted by
neighborhood.

**"Ask Jack."** People submit questions, he answers, each answer becomes a page Google
finds. Slow burn, compounds forever.

**A map of every home he's ever sold.** All 25 years, one map, dots everywhere. No
interactivity needed. Says "I've been here a long time" louder than any paragraph.

### Toys and weird stuff

**Neighborhood Personality Quiz.** Eight questions, tells you which community you belong
in. Shareable, quietly qualifies leads.

**"Price this house" as a group game.** Guess the Sold Price, but he hosts it live at an
open house or on video.

**A sound map.** Click a neighborhood, hear it — trains, freeway, birds, kids.
Completely impractical. People will send it to friends.

**Print pieces that match the site.** Door hangers, sign riders with QR codes, a mailer
that looks like the newsletter. Proves the physical world too.

- Listing presentation deck on demand

- Newsletter archive — Jack is hands-on, cater to him first
- Open house sign-in
- Guess the Sold Price — fun, teaches people, Jack can help build it
