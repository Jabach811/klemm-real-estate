Where the channel stands

You built the asset. It just isn't plugged in.

Five years, 838 listing videos, never a month off. Almost no agent gets that far. But nothing was ever set up around the videos, so YouTube doesn't know who to show them to, and viewers have nowhere to go next. This is what's there, what it's doing, and what it would take to make it work.

What the numbers say

More videos are going up. Each one reaches about the same twenty people.

The pace has picked back up: 176 videos in the last twelve months. But views per video haven't followed. The best year for reach was 2024, when fewer videos went up. Volume isn't the problem and more volume won't be the fix.

How views are spread

Two out of three videos never reached thirty people.

268 videos are under ten views. That's the seller, a couple of friends, and Jack checking the upload. Only 46 videos, about one in eighteen, ever cracked a hundred. The channel isn't reaching strangers, and strangers are the whole point.

What already worked

The channel has already run the experiment. The results are in.

Every one of the top five is from 2021, and four of them are four-to-five minute walkthroughs. They got twenty to a hundred times the views of today's 1:39 slideshows. The third most watched video Jack ever posted isn't a listing at all. It's a one-minute look at the Ellis neighborhood in Tracy. He made exactly one of those, and it beat 835 listing videos.

Next to Jack's most popular video, YouTube recommends "Why Tracy CA is INCREDIBLE" and "Tracy Hills FULL TOUR," both from other agents. That's the lane. Nobody local owns it well yet.

Where the videos are

Six in ten are Tracy. Mountain House is the growth story with the fewest videos.

Why it isn't reaching anyone

The videos are fine. Everything around them is empty.

YouTube decides who sees a video from the words and settings around it: the title, the description, the tags, the playlists, the channel keywords. Almost none of that exists here. Next to a Klemm tour, YouTube currently recommends wallpaper and screensaver videos. It genuinely has no idea what the channel is about.

The first line of a description is the only part people see without clicking "more." Right now that line is never a link. Every future video gets this format automatically once upload defaults are set. It costs nothing per video.

The fix

Three layers. The first two need no new video at all.

1

Fix the room

Settings and copy in YouTube Studio. About a day. Nothing to film.
  • Banner, a real handle (@klemmrealestate), About text, links to all four sites, channel keywords, a featured video.
  • Playlists by city, so 838 videos become a browsable catalog instead of a pile.
  • One bulk edit adds a contact-and-link block to every old description at once.
  • Retitle the 49 recent videos so they lead with the city and "home for sale."
  • End screens on the 46 videos that actually get traffic.
  • Upload defaults, so every future video comes out right without anyone thinking about it.
What this gets you: YouTube finally knows what the channel is about and who to show it to. Anyone who lands on one video can find the rest, and every video has a way to reach you.
2

Wire it into the new website

Part of the site build already underway.
  • Every listing page plays its own tour. Every city page shows that city's recent tours.
  • The Sell page says "every home I list gets its own video tour" and proves it. That's a real advantage over other agents, and it has never once been used as a selling point.
  • Every video description links back to its listing page.
  • klemmre.com becomes the channel's official connected website.
What this gets you: Measurement, for the first time. YouTube Studio shows clicks from videos to the site. The site shows who arrived from YouTube and what they did next. Right now there is no way to know whether a single view has ever turned into a phone call.
3

Get more out of what's already shot

Ongoing, in post. Joel edits. Jack keeps doing what he does now.
  • Titles, descriptions, tags and a consistent thumbnail on every new upload. City and price on the thumbnail, every time.
  • Neighborhood compilations cut from the 838 slideshows already on the channel: "Homes we've sold in Tracy Hills," "River Islands, five years of sales." No new footage.
  • Monthly market update per city, built from the newsletter Jack already writes. He records five minutes of voice on his phone. Joel lays it over charts and photos.
  • A listing video template so each new upload comes out the same in minutes: intro card, captions, end screen.
  • Later, if the above is running: 30-second vertical cuts of each slideshow as Shorts.
What this gets you: The kind of video that already proved it works here. Area content catches people searching "moving to Tracy" or "Mountain House homes." Those people become buyers and sellers. Listing videos never will; they're for the people already looking at that house.
