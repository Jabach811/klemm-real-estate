Why Mountain House

Most towns just happen. Mountain House was designed.

A city built village by village

Every village was planned around its own K-8 school and its own park, so kids walk to class and every front door is a short stroll from green space. The early families sat at kitchen islands before the walls went up, then watched the neighbors' houses rise next door. On the newest streets, that's still happening.

The Bay Area's front porch

Mountain House sits at the very edge of San Joaquin County — the first exit after the Altamont, with the turbines turning on the ridgeline behind it. For families coming out of Hayward, Fremont, and the East Bay, it's Central Valley room without giving up the Bay Area commute.

And now, officially a city

The first residents drove to Tracy or Livermore for groceries and gas. Not anymore — the shops, the fuel, the daily errands are all in town now, and in 2024 residents voted to make it official: California's newest city. Jack watched it happen one sold sign at a time.

Life on the greenbelts

Turn left, turn right. It's a new walk every time.

Push a stroller out the front door, pick a few turns, and you end up somewhere new — a different park, a different stretch of greenbelt, a different playground. The paths thread every village together, and evenings here happen on them.

Ask anyone who has moved away what they miss. It's this. That's the story buyers feel on the first drive through, and it's the story Jack knows how to tell when you sell.

Jack knows your village

Mountain House isn't one market — each village has its own school, its own park, and its own buyers. A Wicklund original and a brand-new build sell differently. Jack prices by village, not ZIP code.
